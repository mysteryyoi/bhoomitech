# Photo collage: test spread 1, "It starts with the land"

**For:** Rohit, who generates the images in ChatGPT and takes the farmer photos. Claude cuts, layers, animates and builds the spread.
**Written:** 28 Nov 2026.
**Why:** the first scrapbook looked thin and cartoonish. It was flat drawings on empty paper. This test rebuilds one spread as a full, layered photo collage, like Diesel Farm's. If it works, the other spreads follow the same recipe.

---

## The rules for this look

1. **No empty paper.** A photo backdrop fills the screen edge to edge, and pieces overlap the edges and reach into the next spread.
2. **Four layers, each moving at its own speed as you scroll:**

   | Layer | Contents | Speed |
   |---|---|---|
   | Backdrop | Sky, horizon, distant fields | Slowest |
   | Middle | Paddy, the bund, palms, the pump house, egrets | Medium |
   | Hero | The farmer and the soil he's turning | Normal |
   | Foreground | Seedling bundles, rice leaves, a water pot, slippers, paper bits | Fastest, and overlapping the edges |

3. **About 20 pieces in the spread,** each one saying something about the place. None are random.
4. **Honest images:**
   - Generated images are fine for sky, land, plants, animals, tools and objects.
   - Anything that is Bhoomi Tech's own must be a real photo. That means people, the farm and the produce.
   - So the farmer is photographed, not generated.
5. **One light, one moment.** Everything is early morning, the sun low on the **left**, soft haze, warm natural colour and a little film grain. Pieces that don't match look pasted in the bad way.

Claude adds the cream cut-paper edge, the shadows, the tape and the text, so **don't ask ChatGPT for borders, shadows or words**.

---

## How to generate (ChatGPT)

- **Size:** use the largest size ChatGPT offers: wide for backdrops, square or tall for objects.
- **Backgrounds:**
  - For a **cut-out**, ask for a **"PNG with a transparent background"**.
  - Check the download really is transparent. If it isn't, say so and Claude will cut it out.
- **Consistency:** paste the style lock below at the start of every prompt.
- **Delivery:**
  - Save into `B:\bhoomi tech website\assets-inbox\photo-collage\spread-1\`.
  - Name each file by its ID and start the name with `ai-`, for example `ai-P1.01-sky.png`.
  - Real photos start with `real-`.
- **Variants:** make 2 or 3 of each and keep the best. Variants are cheap, and picking is where the quality comes from.

**Style lock (paste first, every time):**

> Photorealistic documentary photograph. Kerala, India, monsoon-green paddy country. Early morning, the sun low on the left, soft golden light and light haze. Natural, slightly warm colours, gentle film grain, shot on a 35 mm lens. No people, no text, no logos, no watermark.

## The real photos: the farmer (P1.10)

This is the hero, and it's the only part that has to be photographed. A phone is enough.

- **Who:** a real farmer who is happy to be on the website, with a **signed release** for web and social use. Keep it with the photos.
- **Where:** at the edge of a paddy field, in the early morning, with the sun behind the camera's left shoulder.
- **Framing:**
  - Side view, with him facing **right**, his whole body and the whole mammoty in frame.
  - Landscape orientation.
  - Open sky or a plain wall behind him if you can, which makes the cut-out much cleaner.
- **The swing:** phone on a stand, nothing moving but him. Either:
  - a **burst of photos** through 3 or 4 swings, or
  - a **slow-motion video** (60 fps or more) of the swing.

  Claude picks the frames (lift, top, strike, pull) and turns them into a stop-motion loop, which suits the scrapbook and stays a real photo.
- **Also, if you can:**
  - a close-up of his hands on the handle
  - his feet in the mud
  - the mammoty blade going into the soil

  These are strong foreground pieces.

Until these photos exist, the spread uses a plain silhouette in his place. It never uses a generated person.

## What Claude does with the files

1. Cuts out anything that came back with a background, and cleans the edges.
2. Matches the colour grade across all pieces, so they look like one morning.
3. Adds the cream cut-paper edge and the shadows to every cut-out, then exports WebP at two sizes (laptop and phone).
4. Lays the spread out full-bleed in four layers, with the foreground overlapping the edges.
5. Adds the movement:
   - the rice and palms sway, and the water shimmers
   - the egret pecks
   - the farmer's stop-motion swing
   - scroll depth across all four layers

   Small loops are done in code, rigs in Rive.
6. Builds it at a separate local preview first, so the current live preview stays as it is until you approve.

**Weight budget for this spread:** about 1 MB of images on a laptop, less on phones, loaded only as you approach the spread.
